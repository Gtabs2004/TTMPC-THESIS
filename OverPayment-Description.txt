Loan Overpayment Process

1. Regular Payment Allocation:
   - Each month, the member makes a payment equal to their scheduled monthly amortization.
   - This payment is split into two parts: principal and interest, based on the original amortization schedule.
   - The principal portion reduces the loan balance, while the interest portion is the cost of borrowing.

2. Overpayment Allocation: 
   - When a member pays more than their scheduled monthly amortization, the extra amount is considered an overpayment.
   - The overpayment is applied entirely to the loan principal.
   - This reduces the remaining loan balance by the overpayment amount.

3. Impact on Loan Term:
   - By applying overpayments to the principal, the loan balance is reduced faster than originally scheduled.
   - This effectively shortens the loan term, as the loan will be paid off sooner.
   - However, the number of months and payment dates are not recalculated. The loan is simply paid off once the balance reaches zero.

4. Monthly Payment Amount:
   - The scheduled monthly amortization does not change, even with overpayments.
   - The member continues to pay the same fixed amount each month, as per the original schedule.
   - The allocation of this amount between principal and interest also remains the same, based on the original amortization table.

5. Interest Savings:
   - By reducing the loan balance faster through overpayments, the total interest paid over the life of the loan decreases.
   - This is because interest is charged on the outstanding principal balance, which is lower each month due to the overpayments.
   - However, the monthly interest charge and allocation does not change from the original schedule. The savings come from fewer total payments.

In essence, overpayments are applied directly to the loan principal, reducing the balance and shortening the loan term. The monthly payment amount and principal/interest allocation remain the same as originally scheduled. The member benefits by paying less total interest over a shorter loan term
